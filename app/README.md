# 3 · App

The reveal: Vite + TypeScript + three.js + GSAP. A pack is ripped and the car is revealed in three beats: **year** (a backlit silhouette), **make + rarity** (paint floods in, rarity colour and effects scale with the tier), and **the car**. In the last beat it spins, settles into the exact pose of the toy in its blister, and crossfades into the real pack photo.

```sh
cd app
npm install
npm run dev -- --port 5199     # http://localhost:5199/?car=skyline-hck01
npm run build                  # static site in dist/
```

A top bar on every page links the user-facing pages: **Rip a pack** (the reveal, with a car picker and *Random pack*), **Inspect cars** (`/inspect.html`), **Drive** (`/drive.html`, see below) and **Race online** (`/online.html`, multiplayer: see *Multiplayer* below). Following a link keeps the car you are on. On a phone, a bar with the car picker leaves out *Inspect cars* (no room for it). The bar hides itself in the `mode=` screenshot views and inside the inspector's embedded reveal.

The app serves `asset-generation/assets/` directly (`vite.config.ts` → `publicDir`), so `/cars/<id>/…` is whatever stage 2 last built. `npm run build` copies that whole folder into `dist/`, including the review files and Tripo's FBX downloads. Prune them before deploying anywhere public.

## Two kinds of car

- **Modelled** (`model.glb` + `alignment.json` from stage 2): the full 360° reveal. `src/model.ts` loads the GLB, swaps in the reveal material and glues the pack photo onto the model through the landing pose. That projected photo is what makes the hand-off seamless. The GLB can come from the `ripcars` scripts or be a promoted Tripo mesh; either way its wheels are `wheel_*` pivot nodes, which the reveal spins whole turns, so they land back at rest on the photo. Windows are the texels marked as glass in its roughness map; `src/glass.ts` makes them see-through.
- **Procedural fallback** (no `model.glb`, needs `params.json`): `src/car.ts` builds a car from the pack-photo outline alone. It reveals near side-on and morphs from a generic coupe into the car. Force it with `?reveal=profile`.

## Drive page

`/drive.html?car=<id>` takes the car for a spin on one of two courses, picked with `?track=` or the *Meadow | Sky Tower* switch in the top bar (under 1200 px wide it hangs below the bar's right end):

- **Meadow** (the default; `?track=meadow` or no `track`): a 2,740 u lap for a 4 u car, about 65 s. Twisty asphalt roads with mown grass verges, tyre walls and hay bales, a loose dirt stretch, and orange Hot Wheels plastic for the set pieces only - the raised start gate and its drop, a small kicker over a gap on scaffolding, the big jump (a booster up a steep ramp on a tall scaffold tower, about 2.3 s of air over the road below, a long landing ramp; a pond and a RIPCARS banner beside it), a booster into the loop and a banked climb on stone pillars back to the gate.
- **Sky Tower** (`?track=sky`): track 2, the crazy one - the Hot Wheels Sky Crash Tower set with a Trackmania edge. The gate sits high on a tall blue lattice tower; the plunge drops off it in the narrow classic channel straight into a loop, then come chained boosters, a steeply banked 180, a double jump and the wall ride - a 180 banked at 70° that you drive up onto from the meadow, with one narrow booster pad in the middle of its approach (line up for it, or ride the wall slower) - then a single rounded crest in the straight off the wall ride (1.6 u high, 32 u long), esses and a chicane where drifting pays, a rhythm section of two long rollers on the wide straight east to the climb (2 u high, 46 u crest to crest; `SKY_BUMPS` in `tracks/sky.ts`), the climb (a row of chained pads into a ramp that goes up past vertical, over the top upside down and rolls out into a banked right-hander onto a deck 41 u up - Hot Wheels' up the wall and over: it's the pads' speed that carries you, and a car that comes to it too slow falls off and is put back before the pads; the right-hander tightens to an 18 u radius as it narrows to 11 u, a drift corner), the sky bridge (the narrow deck flares and splits into two lanes, 9.5 u each, either side of an island - a gap you see the meadow through, its striped points walls like any rail - and each lane has its own jump: the left one early and steeper, ~1.6-1.7 s of air, the right one later and flatter, ~1.5-1.6 s; both lanes widen outward into catch trays over the landings and lap within a few hundredths of each other; you take the lane on the side you're on at the island's point, and a miss puts you back in that lane, lined up, before its jump; `BRIDGE` in `tracks/sky.ts`, the split itself in `tracks/split.ts`), the sky jump's in-run (the deck runs on north, and off its far end the dive turns right and down: a 270° descending right-hander, banked 20°, that comes back round and passes UNDER the deck - the classic Hot Wheels over/under, ~16 u of headroom, the deck's stilts standing clear of it - lines up, then plunges at 11° where the speed builds, to a booster pad at its foot) and the sky jump itself (a short steep kicker low down, up and over a gap through a hoop onto a long ski-slope landing hill: the biggest air in the game, ~3.3 s; land early near its top or further down - anything from 0.85 to 1.15 of the run-up's speed lands lined up; manual like the meadow's big jump, so a straight hit lands perfect), and the spiral climbing round the tower back up to the start. It is built for racing side by side: most of the lap is wide enough for three or four cars abreast (the meadow's plastic channel takes two), narrowing to the classic channel only where the squeeze is the point (the plunge and its loop) and to the small toy tracks of the sky bridge, with a start grid for 8 cars at the gate.

A course is one file in `src/drive/tracks/` exporting a `Course` (`tracks/course.ts`): its id, name, blurb and colour, its layout (written with the `Path` pieces in `tracks/path.ts`, two free straights closing the lap), the start line, its own support kinds and set dressing, the world style, and its medal pace (`pars`, measured with the autopilot). Adding track 3 is that file plus one entry in `COURSES` (`tracks/index.ts`); the track switch, *Try*, the bests' keys and the medals pick it up from there.

Three laps, splits against your best lap, results with a medal, *Again*, *Rip a pack* and *Try* the other course. Bests (and the air record) are kept per car and course in `localStorage`: `ripcars.drive.<car>.<lap length>` on the meadow (the key it had before track 2, so earlier bests stand) and `ripcars.drive.<car>.sky.<lap length>` on the Sky Tower; a layout with a new length starts afresh. Changing course reloads the page with the same car and URL knobs (`auto`, `laps`, ...); the bar's car picker and *Random car* keep the course. The reveal's *Take it for a spin* button and the top bar's *Drive* link from the other pages open the meadow for the car you are on. The car waits in the start gate under the course's name; the first key, tap or pad button drops it and unlocks the sound.

- **Keys**: W / ↑ gas, S / ↓ brake and reverse, A D / ← → steer, Space or Shift drift, R restart, M mute. **Pad**: stick, RT / LT, A or RB drift, Start restart.
- **Touch**: the car drives itself; drag anywhere to steer. Hold full lock (or tap a second finger) to drift; centre the thumb or lift it and the car straightens, which ends the drift and fires the boost.
- A drift is a tap, not a hold: the rear goes loose and the slide is yours - steer to hold it, counter-steer or lift to catch it. It ends by itself once the car is straight again (or at a second tap). Slide long enough and the sparks go blue, purple, then the car's tier colour (0.9, 1.8 and 2.7 s of real sliding), and a slide kept going twice as long as gold, linked through a whole S, hits a hidden fourth: the *magic max*, with its own sparks, chime and a longer, stronger boost. A drift that ends clean fires a boost that grows with the tier (a wall hit or a spin cancels it). Land a jump straight for a PERFECT boost. Booster pads, two jumps and a loop are on the meadow's lap. The big jump flies off whatever you bring to the lip - hit the booster fast and straight, leave the wheel alone in the air for a PERFECT; miss and you're put back before the run-up.
- **Boosts stack.** Every boost - a booster pad (1 s of push, plus its instant +8 u/s kick), a drift's mini-turbo (0.5 / 1 / 1.5 s by tier, the magic max's 2.5 s and 1.3x stronger) and a PERFECT landing (0.5 s) - pours its time into one shared tank, so nothing is wasted: a big drift cashed in just before a pad runs on after the pad's push. The tank burns its strongest push first (pad and landing, then the magic max's, then a mini-turbo's), so a lone boost pushes exactly as it always did. Each boost added while the tank is still running is a stack (`state.boostStack`; no text pops up: the flames, sound and speed show it): it raises the boost's top speed by 4 u/s (56 alone, 60 at x2, 64 at x3 ...), fading back over the tank's last second; the stack is gone once the tank is empty. There is no cap on the tank or the stacks - a run of pads throws the car as fast and as far as the physics says, and stacking into a jump can carry it past the landing (then the usual miss rules put it back). The flames burn longer, wider and hotter and the boost hiss and engine get louder per stack. The Sky Tower's chained pads stack to x3 on every lap (the meadow's pads are too far apart).
- **Air records**: the longest landed flight (0.9 s or more) is kept per car and course with the bests (the same `localStorage` key, as `air`); beating one from an earlier race says so as the car lands (*Big air 4.6 s, record!*), and the results card shows the race's longest air against the record.
- Each car drives and sounds like itself (`PROFILES` in `vehicles/arcade.ts`, multipliers on the shared tuning; `ENGINES` in `audio.ts`), and medals are judged against each car's own pace on each course (each course's `pars` in its `tracks/<course>.ts`: the autopilot's average speed over 3 laps; a course without a car's number uses its meadow pace, so the par time scales with the lap's length):
  - **Porsche 911 GT3 R** (Roxy): the most grip, the hardest brakes, the sharpest steering and the highest top speed, but hard to get sideways and slow on dirt. A flat-6 race wail with crackles on the lift.
  - **Skyline LB-ER34**: a breath of turbo lag, then the strongest mid-range pull; the easiest, most controllable drifter. An RB26 inline-6 with turbo whistle and a blow-off on every lift.
  - **1964 Impala**: a heavy, soft boat with a lower top speed, lazy steering and big floaty slides, but at home on the dirt and the one that carries a hill's speed furthest. A big-block V8 burble through a lazy 3-speed automatic.
  - **Porsche 911 GT3 RS**: the road-going racer, a step below the GT3 R on grip, with a flat-6 that revs to 8,400 through a 6-speed manual.
  - **Porsche 964**: an air-cooled classic 911 whose tail swings readily on a lift. A dry, raspy flat-6 and a 5-speed.
  - **'77 Firebird Trans Am**: a muscle car that pulls hard out of corners, fades up top and steps its tail out under power. An angry V8 through a 4-speed.
  - **Beach Bomb Too**: the VW bus with surfboards, the slowest car, tall, soft and floaty, but happy on the dirt. An air-cooled flat-4 putter.
  - **'99 Civic Type R**: a light front-drive hot hatch, nimble and grippy but hard to get sideways. A VTEC four screaming to 8,400.
  - **'93 Mustang Cobra R**: a Fox-body V8 with a strong low-down shove and a willing tail. A 5.8 V8 through a 5-speed.
  - **Godzilla, green and gold Skylines** drive and sound like the other Skyline (same RB26). A car without its own entry drives the stock car and gets an engine by make.
- Drive against the lap and a WRONG WAY sign comes up; after 4 s of it, or 4 s stuck, the car is put back on the track facing the right way. A missed jump puts it back well before the booster.

The driving model is hand-written (`src/drive/vehicles/arcade.ts`, no physics engine): the car lives in track space, gravity is projected onto the track so slopes, banks and the loop just work, and it flies off any crest it's too fast to stay on (see *Crests and bumps* above). Each surface grips and tops out differently (plastic fastest, asphalt grippiest, dirt loose and bumpy, the grass verge slow and slippery). The throttle pushes only up to the car's top speed; past it only the air drags, gently, so a hill (the start drop, the big jump's landing ramp) carries the car well over its top speed into the next corners, where the tyres grip no harder than at top speed: brake, or take a wider line. Walls push back at the corner that touches, with the restitution and friction of what they are made of (plastic rail, tyre wall, hay bales): a glancing hit scrapes along, a hard one stuns for a moment, a very hard one spins the car out (hay bales only swallow the hit and slow the car, never spin it), and the car never sticks to a wall. The simulation runs in fixed 1/120 s steps and is deterministic and input-driven - no randomness, no clocks, and game time never slows or stops (no slow motion, no hit-stop), so it can run in lockstep for multiplayer later; a hard hit's drama is the camera's kick, shake and FOV punch, the sound, the sparks and the haptics. Everything is generated in code, sounds included; the only downloads are the car's GLB and JSON.

**Crests and bumps throw the car**: it leaves the track wherever the track can't hold it at its speed - the same test as the loop's (gravity into the surface against the speed's pull off a crest), with only a little toy-track magnet (`hopStick`, 0.15 g) - flies the same ballistics and lands with the same landings. So the Sky Tower's bumps throw it: at race speed it hops from crest to crest through the rhythm section (two long rollers, ~0.5 s of air each at race speed) and flies ~0.7 s off the crest after the wall ride; slower, it rolls over them. A hop is quiet: it shows as airborne (dust, sound, camera) once it's 0.1 s or 0.25 u clear of the track, it isn't steered (the wheel held through it doesn't twist the car: it lands straight), and only a flight of 0.9 s or more is graded (PERFECT, ok, bad). Banked turns (over 15°) hold the car, there's no new hop within 0.1 s of a landing (no buzz), and the set pieces fly, fall or hold as built: both big jumps (in-run, lip, gap, landing ramp), the kickers, the loops, the climb, and the start drops (the Sky Tower's plunge would throw a car 1.3-1.7 s down the tower into the loop's mouth, so it holds). The meadow has no crest sharp enough to throw a car at its speeds. It's all deterministic.

## Multiplayer (local demo)

Race other tabs on one machine (or others on your network) against a small server that runs the game's own simulation.

```sh
cd app
npm run dev -- --port 5199     # the page, as usual
npm run server                 # the drive server: ws://localhost:8790 (stats: http://localhost:8790/stats)
```

**Playing from the UI** (no URL to type):

1. **Race online** in the top bar (or *Race it online* next to *Take it for a spin* at the end of a reveal) opens `/online.html`, the room browser: your name and car (both remembered), then the open rooms - name, track, laps, players x/10 with their names and cars (★ the host), and whether it's waiting, starting or racing - refreshed every 2 s, each with **Join** (a racing room: you're in its next race; a full one says *Full*). With no rooms it says how to start; when the game server isn't answering it says so, with `npm run server` to start it.
2. **Create room**: a name (filled in, editable; a name that's already open is refused, with a link to join it), the track (Meadow / Sky Tower, with their colour dots), laps (1, 2, 3, 5) and *Fill with bots* (off, 2, 4, 6 server-driven cars that race like decent players: *Bots* below). It opens the room.
3. **In the room** (the drive page, the car on its grid slot behind the card): the course and laps, the room's name, **Copy invite link** (a link that drops a friend straight into this room with their own name and car), the players with their car, Ready state and ping (*You*, *Host*: the room's first player, then the next in join order), your name and car - changed in place, everyone's list follows (a car change un-readies you; between races it takes effect back in the lobby; the top bar's car picker does the same) - a big **Ready** (or Enter), a line saying what it's waiting for, and *Leave room* (back to the browser). When everyone is ready: 3-2-1 and the gate drops for all at the same step.
4. **Race**: the top right shows your position, the standings and your ping; the other cars carry name tags - and you can crash into them (*Crashing into each other* below). Join mid-race and the card says you're in the next one.
5. **Results**: the finish order with the winner's time and everyone's gap (DNF 30 s after the winner), **Race again** (back to this room's lobby) or **Leave**. The results stay up until you choose; the room goes back to its lobby 8 s after the race, or as soon as every player has pressed Ready again.

`drive.html` without `online=1` is the single-player page exactly as before (nothing of the online code loads). The URL still works by hand (handy for tests): `http://localhost:5199/drive.html?online=1&room=demo&name=Ann&car=skyline-hck01&track=sky`, and the same with `name=Bob` in a second tab - the invite link is this URL without the name and car. `GET http://localhost:8790/rooms` is the browser's list as JSON.

| Query (with `online=1`) | Does |
| --- | --- |
| `room=<name>` | the room (made on the first join; default `demo`). Its first player sets the course and laps; joining it with another `track`/`laps` reloads on the room's |
| `name=<you>` | your name (remembered); `car=`, `track=`, `laps=`, `auto=1`, `fps=1` as offline |
| `auto=1` | the autopilot drives your car (and readies you): handy for testing with several tabs |
| `lag=<ms>` `jitter=<ms>` `loss=<%>` | a bad network simulated in the page: `lag` is added to the round trip (half each way), each message up to `jitter` more (order kept, as TCP keeps it), `loss` drops that share of snapshots and input packets - to see the smoothing |
| `min=<n>` | the room waits for n players before a race can start |
| `bots=<n>` | a new room adds n server-side bots (any number of humans can still join) |
| `server=ws://host:port` | another server (default: this host, port 8790) |

**Crashing into each other** is the game's own physics, the same on the server and in every page, tuned to feel like solid toy cars, not pinballs: two cars meet as real impacts between their boxes - each car's own footprint (the box that hits the walls) standing as tall as its model, its mass its profile's heft (the Impala 1.35 and the Beach Bomb 1.3 shove the Civic 0.85 and the Porsches 0.9 about), its yaw inertia 2.5 x a box's (the tyres resist being turned). The hit lands at the corner or edge that touches, with a dull knock rather than a bounce (restitution 0.1; the rails' is 0.3), friction along the flanks (0.25) and the turn that impulse makes about each car's middle, at most 1.5 rad/s a step (as a wall's kick is capped) - so a door-to-door nudge moves the other car a few tenths of a unit and it keeps control, a 20 u/s T-bone knocks it a couple of units sideways with a wobble, a heavy car rear-ending a light one pushes it on (bump drafting), a head-on stops both, two cars side by side trade paint and lean on each other, and only a big T-bone on the rear quarter or a real PIT at speed spins a car round. A push across a car's nose is a sideways slide its tyres scrub off at 36 u/s^2 (it smokes and squeals), never turned into speed along the nose; pushes build that slide up to 12 u/s at most (one hard hit knocks a car as hard as it is), and the slide keeps its way in the world while the car turns under it (a car spun by a hit slides on the way it was sent). A car pressed against a rail can't be pushed further into it: the rail takes that part of the push and the car pushing takes the whole bounce - being sandwiched never launches you off the rail or slams you into it. The walls' own rules judge a hit: its speed times the other car's share of the mass (the wall it was as good as) ends a drift, stuns and spins out at the walls' thresholds; a hit behind a car's middle, square on and as hard as a 14 u/s wall hit (28 u/s between two cars of the same heft), knocks the tail out and spins it; and pushes on the rear quarter that close faster than a touch build up yaw - past 3 rad/s at over 30 u/s, a PIT, it spins. Nudges, scrapes and resting contact (door to door, nose to tail) never spin anyone. It works on banks, round the loops, up the wall ride and in the air (cars knock each other off line mid-flight; one coming down on another's roof bounces off it), never through a split's island, and not with a car that was just put back on the track (it passes through for a second) or one that has finished. A crash plays like a wall hit: sparks at the contact point, a metal bang by severity (the heavy thud and the boom on a hard one) or a grinding scrape, the camera's kick, shake and lens punch toward the car that hit, rumble; the other cars' crashes show their sparks and a knock by distance.

**Bots** (*Fill with bots*, `bots=<n>`) are the game's autopilot driven on the server, every input through the same quantize and checks as a player's, racing in traffic (`input.ts` `createAutopilot({ traffic })`; `server/sim.ts` hands each bot the race as it stands at the start of the step - every car's place, speed and size): off the grid a bot keeps its lane and eases toward the line (no dive across the pack), and it picks its line across the road among slots a quarter of a unit apart - near the line and where it's steering already, 0.8 u clear of the cars beside it (never across one), clear of the ones it's catching where they'll be - inside the narrowest the road gets over the next ~0.9 s, so a funnel ahead (the Sky Tower's plunge) squeezes it in early. Boxed in with no slot clear, it gives way to the car that has the place - ahead, or level and nearer the line - by lifting, and braking while the road closes in, and tucks in behind (the pack zips into the plunge two abreast: the inner slots first); a car ahead in its path that it's catching, it follows at a gap (1 u + 0.1 s), easing off the throttle smoothly rather than running into it, unless it's going round it; no drift with a car close by. It bumps now and then, it never rams. Alone it laps exactly as the plain autopilot (within 0.01% over 3 laps, every car, both courses); deterministic (a function of the race's state); single player and `?auto=1` don't use any of it (bit-identical).

**How it works** (`src/drive/net/`, `server/`):

- **Authority**: the server (TypeScript on Node, `server/index.ts`) runs the same code as the page - `vehicles/arcade.ts` on the `tracks/` course, then car-to-car contact (`net/contact.ts`) - for every car at 120 Hz. Clients send only their inputs; nothing a client says about its position is trusted. The hot messages are binary (`net/codec.ts`: 60 Hz snapshots down, inputs up); the rest is JSON (`net/protocol.ts`: join, room, laps, results, pings).
- **Inputs**: each step's input is quantized (steer -1000..1000, throttle and brake 0..1000, drift 0/1) and tagged with its step; a packet carries every input the server hasn't acknowledged yet (the last 12 steps at most), the first whole (5 bytes) and each next as what changed (a byte, plus a varint per changed value: a held key costs a byte a step), and the hashes of the client's predicted states at the snapshot steps it hasn't reported yet. The client drives its own car on exactly the quantized numbers the server will use.
- **Clock**: the client runs its own step counter a little ahead of the server's, so its input for step k arrives before the server simulates k. Each snapshot says how early the newest input arrived; the client's clock steers that (the worst over the last 2 s) to 2 steps - the server's **jitter buffer** - by running up to 3% fast or slow, and jumps when far off (the steps a stalled frame still owes count, so the snapshots read after it don't each add the jump). An input that still arrives late is dropped and the server repeats the player's last one (counted as `late`). The server's own clock is hrtime: each step has its moment, the timer is set for the next one, a late wake runs the steps owed (never drifting), half a second behind drops the backlog.
- **Snapshots**, 60 Hz (every 2 steps), binary, one per client: every other car's **net state** (`arcade.ts` `netState()`: 72 numbers - where it is on the track, heading, slip, speed, yaw, the knock a hit left, the input its player is on (the newest the server has), the surface under it, its drift, boost tank and timers, its flight in the air, its laps and finish), each quantized to its own unit (`NET_SCALE`: 1/4096 u along the track, 1/16384 rad, 1/256 u/s ...) and sent as the change since the last snapshot - the WebSocket is TCP, so the last one is always there: only the changed fields, grouped by eight with a bit per changed group and per changed field, zig-zag varints. A cruising car costs ~15-25 bytes, a parked one 3; every client has had the same last snapshot, so a car's change is encoded once and the same bytes go to all (a client new to a car gets it whole). Then your race when it changes (laps, lap start, finish), the ack and how early your inputs arrive, whether your predicted state was right - and when it wasn't, your car's exact state: the 77 numbers of its simulation (`CORE_LENGTH`; the state's outputs follow from them exactly: `restoreCore()`), as the XOR change from the last one sent (unchanged numbers cost a bit, a number that moved a little ~6 bytes), at most 20 times a second (a client in a pack, wrong all the time, is told it was wrong in between).
- **Prediction - every car, in your own timeline** (`net/world.ts`, run by `net/client.ts`): your car moves the moment you press; every other car is simulated forward from its last snapshot on the input its player is on - the sim is deterministic, so that's exact until they change input - with car-to-car contact predicted against them, in the server's order (the grid slots). A crash happens on your screen the moment you make it, against cars drawn where they are now: nobody is drawn in the past, so a car beside you is beside you. A prediction that would put another car back on the track (a miss the server may not have seen) waits for the server instead: respawns are the server's to say.
- **Reconciliation**: each snapshot rewinds the whole world to the server's tick - your car to its exact state there (the server's, when your prediction missed; else the state you predicted and it confirmed), the others to the snapshot's - and replays to now. Each car keeps what's on screen where it was: the difference goes into its visual error, which decays (yours with a 60 ms time constant, ~95% gone in 180 ms; the others' 80 ms); a correction over 6 u (a respawn) snaps. A crash only the replay found plays late (its sparks and sound), unless one just played.
- **Car-to-car contact** (`net/contact.ts`, after every step, on the server and in the page): pairs close along the track (12 u) and in the world, in the same lane, neither a ghost; the two boxes are tested in the plane their up vectors share (separating axes on their 4 in-plane axes, plus their overlap along the plane's normal: a car in the air passes over one below unless they really meet). The contact normal is the axis they came together on - swept: the axis they overlapped on last, from where they were a step ago, so a 120 u/s car can't pass through another - or the least overlap for two that were already touching; the contact point is the incident face clipped against the reference face (a corner hit is at the corner; two flanks side by side touch in the middle of their overlap). The impulse: closing speed bounced back by 0.1 (none under 3 u/s or for a glancing scrape under 15 deg, fading in over 10 deg, as the walls), Coulomb friction 0.25, effective mass with both cars' yaw inertia (2.5 x a box's) about the contact, a step's yaw at most 1.5 rad/s a car; a car pressed against a rail takes no push into it - the rail holds that part (fully with its corner on it, fading out over 0.3 u off it: smooth, so a prediction a hair off doesn't push it another way), the other car takes the rest. Overlap is eased apart gently, as a position change only (25% a pass, at most 0.06 u, the heavier car moving less, never into a rail), 3 passes a step so a pack of ten settles without jitter. The step's sum goes to each car's `carHit()`: the stun / spin-out rules and the events the effects read. With one car in the room none of it runs; offline nothing of it loads.
- **Race flow** (`server/room.ts`): a room by name (the lobby browser lists them: `GET /rooms`); players join with a name and a car and take the lowest free grid slot (the Sky Tower's own 8-car grid; on the meadow rows of two, 7 u apart, behind the line; more rows behind for more cars; up to 10); when everyone in the lobby is Ready there's a 3 s countdown and every car starts on the same step (and everyone is un-readied: Ready from the results on asks for the next race). A player can rename (`name`) any time and change car (`car`): in the lobby the server swaps the car on its grid slot at once (the player is un-readied, so no countdown starts under the swap), during a race it waits for the lobby. Laps count by single player's rule, the finish order is by finish step, results are broadcast when everyone is in (or 30 s after the winner: the rest DNF). A car that has finished passes through the others. Respawns and wrong way are the driving model's own, as offline. A player who joins mid-race waits for the next one; one who leaves mid-race drops out.
- **Plausibility checks** (from day one; each counted per player, shown in the lobby, logged): a malformed packet is dropped (`format`); a value out of range is clamped (`range`); the steering may not move faster than the game's own key/pad smoothing allows - 12/s, 100 quantized units per step - against the previous step (`slew`, clamped); an input more than 2 s ahead of the server is dropped (`future`); more new steps than real time allows (126/s, bursts of 60) are dropped (`rate`). `&raw=1` makes a client skip the slew, to see them counted.
- **Race logs**: every race's applied inputs (only where they change), laps, leaves and its end (results, the race's contact counts, violations, a hash of every car's full state) go to `server/logs/<room>/<time>-race<n>.jsonl` (git-ignored; log version 3: the tamer car-to-car contact of `net/contact.ts` - older logs re-simulate only on their own code, `replay.ts` skips them; a bot's inputs are logged like a player's, so a replay doesn't run the bots). `npm run replay -- server/logs/<room>/<file>.jsonl` (`server/replay.ts`) re-simulates a race - every collision included - and checks the results, the contact counts and the state hash against the live race's. `node server/bench.ts <cars> <course> [laps] [race|spread|pack]` times the server's whole step (sim, contact, laps, log and every player's snapshot) with the players on the autopilot: a race from the grid, the cars spread round the lap (no one near anyone), or the worst case - every car aiming for the same spot all the time, a pack shoving constantly.
- **Stats**: `GET http://localhost:8790/stats` - the step time and how late each step ran (histograms), GC pauses, per room the step split (sim, sockets), contact counts, and per player bytes in and out, snapshots and full states sent; `/reset-stats` zeroes them. The page's `window.__online.stats` has its side (corrections, rollbacks, bandwidth).

**Known limits**: a local demo - no accounts, no matchmaking, no security beyond the input checks (anyone who can reach the port can join any room). Another player's car is predicted on the input it was last on, so when they turn or brake you see it a round trip later, smoothed (at 150 ms that's up to ~1 u, p99 0.5); a crash you predicted against a car that turned away may not have happened (its sparks played; the cars are put right). Browser and Node can differ in the last bit of `Math.exp` (same V8, other builds), so the prediction isn't bit-exact for long; the state hash is taken at a millionth of a unit, and the server is the reference for any re-simulation. The other cars have no engine sound or effects of their own beyond their crashes (sparks, flames). A hidden tab stops sending inputs (the server repeats the last one) and resyncs when shown. Car-to-car contact is boxes: a nose or a wing is as square as the rest of the car. A bot is predicted like a player's car, on the input it was last on (it's the server's to drive); a player who drives blind into the pack still crashes - the bots give way only to a car that has the place.

**Next steps**: WebTransport (unreliable datagrams) or WebRTC data channels instead of TCP (the deltas would then go against the last acknowledged snapshot); hosting the rooms on Cloudflare Durable Objects (a room per object, close to the players) or a small Node/Bun box per region; ranked races verified by re-simulating the stored input log on the server (`replay.ts` already does it); bot-detection statistics from the logs (input entropy, reaction times, steering spectra, how close a lap runs to the autopilot's); damage you can see (dents, a loose bumper) from the hits' severity; the bots predicted exactly - their autopilot run in the page too (it's deterministic; its few numbers of state would travel in the snapshot); a staggered grid on the Sky Tower (each car of a row a little behind the one inside it) if players crowd the plunge's funnel - the bots zip into it as it is.

## Review pages (dev server)

- `/inspect.html`: every car in one place: list on the left, then a 3D viewer (clay, wireframe, preset angles), the sheet comparison, the reveal and the build report with its checks and manual retouch notes. The list comes from `asset-generation/assets/cars/index.json`, which every `ripcars` run rewrites.
- `/views.html?car=<id>[&m=<snapshot>]`: each ChatGPT sheet view next to the model, rendered from the exact same angle (raw texture, or tick *lit*). Use it to check shape, texture alignment and windows against the reference. `m` shows a snapshot instead of `model.glb`.
- `/compare.html?car=<id>&m=<a>,<b>,…`: versions of a model side by side, orbiting together. A name is a snapshot in `asset-generation/assets/cars/<id>/snapshots/<name>.glb` or `.fbx` (Tripo's raw FBX is normalised on load). `current` is the app's `model.glb`, and `scripted` is the scripts' model kept by `ripcars promote`. More options:

  | Option | Does |
  | --- | --- |
  | `refs=1` | adds the reference images |
  | `view=az,el,dist[,tx,ty,tz]` | starting camera: radians and model units. `az` 0 = right side, π/2 = front. Example: `0,1.5,7` is from above |
  | `glass=0` | opaque windows, showing exactly what is painted on the glass |
  | `clay=1` | untextured |
  | `fov=2` | near-orthographic |
  | `nogrid=1` | no ground grid |
  | `flip=<name>` | turns one model 180° |
